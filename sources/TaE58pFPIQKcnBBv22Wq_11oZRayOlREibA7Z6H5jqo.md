# Selank (Nootropic and Anxiolytic Peptide) 

## What It Is

Selank is a synthetic peptide derived from tuftsin, a naturally occurring molecule in your immune system. It was developed by the Institute of Molecular Genetics at the Russian Academy of Sciences as an alternative to benzodiazepines for treating anxiety. Unlike traditional anti-anxiety medications, Selank reduces anxiety without causing sedation, cognitive impairment, or dependence.

The peptide has been approved as a prescription medication in Russia since the early 2000s, where it is used to treat generalized anxiety disorder, neurasthenia (chronic fatigue with anxiety), and stress-related conditions. In the United States, it remains a research compound without FDA approval.

What makes Selank interesting is that it works through multiple pathways. It modulates GABA (the brain's primary calming neurotransmitter), influences serotonin and dopamine, supports immune function, and increases BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor). This gives it a unique profile: anxiolytic (anti-anxiety), nootropic (cognitive enhancing), and immunomodulatory all in one compound.

For people dealing with chronic stress, anxiety, or cognitive fatigue, Selank offers a different approach than stimulants or traditional pharmaceuticals. It calms without sedating and sharpens focus without causing jitteriness.




## How It Works

To understand why Selank works, you need to understand the GABAergic system. GABA is your brain's main inhibitory neurotransmitter. When GABA binds to its receptors, it slows down neuronal activity and creates a calming effect. This is the same system that benzodiazepines like Xanax and Valium target.

Selank works as a positive allosteric modulator of GABA receptors. This means it does not bind directly to the GABA receptor the way benzodiazepines do. Instead, it changes the shape of the receptor in a way that makes GABA bind more effectively. The result is enhanced inhibitory signaling without the direct receptor activation that causes sedation and dependence.

Clinical studies have shown that Selank produces anxiolytic effects comparable to benzodiazepines, but without the sedation, memory impairment, or addiction potential. The anxiolytic effect has been shown to persist for up to a week after discontinuing the peptide, suggesting it helps restore balance in the stress response system rather than just masking symptoms.

Beyond GABA, Selank also:

- Increases BDNF in the hippocampus and cortex, supporting memory formation and neuroplasticity
- Modulates serotonin metabolism, contributing to mood stability
- Regulates dopamine and norepinephrine, supporting focus and motivation
- Influences enkephalin activity, which may explain some of its anti-anxiety effects
- Retains immunomodulatory properties from its parent molecule tuftsin

This multi-pathway action is why Selank can reduce anxiety while simultaneously improving cognitive performance. Most anxiolytics impair cognition. Selank does the opposite.




## Benefits

**Anxiety Reduction Without Sedation**

The primary benefit is anxiety relief that does not compromise your ability to function. In clinical trials comparing Selank to phenazepam (a benzodiazepine), Selank produced equivalent anxiety reduction without the sedative effects. You can use it during the day without feeling drowsy or mentally foggy.

**Cognitive Enhancement**

Selank functions as a nootropic, improving memory, attention, and information processing. Animal studies show enhanced learning and memory formation, and human users report improved focus, particularly under stress. The BDNF increase supports synaptic plasticity, which is the foundation of learning and memory.

**Stress Resilience**

By modulating the HPA axis (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis), Selank helps regulate your stress response. With continued use, people report feeling more resilient to daily stressors rather than reactive. This is different from just suppressing anxiety symptoms.

**No Dependence or Withdrawal**

Unlike benzodiazepines, Selank does not cause physical dependence or withdrawal symptoms. You can use it as needed or in cycles without worrying about becoming dependent on it to function.

**Immune Support**

Selank retains some immunomodulatory properties from tuftsin. Research shows it can influence cytokine expression and may have antiviral properties. This is a secondary benefit, but relevant for people dealing with chronic stress, which typically suppresses immune function.




## What the Science Shows

Selank has more human clinical data than most research peptides, primarily from Russian studies.

**Zozulia et al. (2008), Zhurnal Nevrologii i Psikhiatrii**

This study compared Selank to medazepam (a benzodiazepine) in 62 patients with generalized anxiety disorder and neurasthenia. Both groups showed similar reductions in anxiety on the Hamilton and Zung scales. However, Selank also produced anti-asthenic (energy-boosting) and psychostimulant effects that the benzodiazepine did not. The researchers found that patients with GAD had decreased enkephalin levels, and Selank treatment increased these levels while improving symptoms.

**Seredenin et al. (2014), Zhurnal Nevrologii i Psikhiatrii**

A comparative study of Selank versus phenazepam in 60 patients with anxiety disorders. Selank produced pronounced anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects. Notably, the anxiolytic effect lasted for a week after the last dose of the peptide. Selank also improved quality of life scores without the cognitive side effects associated with phenazepam.

**Filatova et al. (2017), Frontiers in Pharmacology**

This study examined how Selank affects gene expression related to GABAergic neurotransmission. Researchers found that Selank administration significantly altered the expression of 45 genes involved in neurotransmission within one hour. The changes were positively correlated with those produced by GABA itself, supporting the hypothesis that Selank works through GABAergic mechanisms.

**Kasian et al. (2019), Current Protein and Peptide Science**

Radioligand binding studies showed that Selank acts as a positive allosteric modulator of GABA receptors. It can compete with diazepam for binding sites but produces different effects, suggesting it binds to a related but distinct site on the GABA receptor complex.




## Dosing Protocol

Selank can be administered either intranasally or subcutaneously. In Russia, it is primarily used as a nasal spray. In the research community, subcutaneous injection is common because it provides more consistent dosing.

**Understanding the Routes**

Intranasal administration allows rapid absorption directly into the brain, bypassing the blood-brain barrier to some degree. This is the traditional route used in Russian clinical practice. Subcutaneous injection provides systemic delivery with predictable absorption. Both routes are effective, and the choice often comes down to personal preference and convenience.

**Anxiolytic and Nootropic Protocol**

For general anxiety reduction and cognitive support:

- Dose: 300 to 500 mcg daily
- Frequency: Once daily
- Route: Subcutaneous or intranasal
- Timing: Morning or early afternoon
- Cycle: 10 to 14 days on, 7 to 14 days off
- Rationale: This aligns with Russian clinical protocols and provides the anxiolytic and nootropic benefits without building tolerance

**Acute Stress or Demanding Periods**

For short-term use during high-stress situations:

- Dose: 200 to 400 mcg
- Frequency: Once or twice daily as needed
- Duration: Use during the stressful period, then discontinue
- Rationale: Selank works quickly (within hours to days) and does not require loading

**Stacking with Semax**

Selank and Semax are commonly used together. Selank provides the calming, anxiolytic effect while Semax provides stimulating, focus-enhancing effects. The combination produces calm focus without sedation or jitteriness.

- Selank: 200 to 300 mcg morning
- Semax: 200 to 400 mcg morning or early afternoon
- Rationale: The compounds work through different mechanisms and complement each other

**Why Cycling Matters**

While Selank does not cause dependence, cycling helps maintain sensitivity and allows you to assess your baseline. The anxiolytic effects often persist after discontinuation, so continuous use is not necessary for sustained benefit.

# Semax (Nootropic Peptide) 

## What It Is

Semax is a synthetic peptide derived from a fragment of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH). Developed in Russia in the 1980s, it has been used as a prescription medication there for decades to treat stroke, traumatic brain injury, cognitive decline, and optic nerve disorders. Unlike many nootropics that work through stimulation, Semax enhances cognition by increasing brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and supporting neuroplasticity.

The name "Semax" comes from "seven amino acids" in Russian (СЕМь АминоКислот). It is a heptapeptide with the sequence Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro. This structure is based on ACTH(4-10), the portion of the hormone associated with cognitive effects, modified for improved stability and brain penetration.

What makes Semax interesting is its dual role: it works as both a nootropic (cognitive enhancer) and a neuroprotectant. In healthy people, it sharpens focus, improves memory, and enhances mental clarity. In people recovering from stroke or brain injury, it accelerates healing and preserves brain function. This combination of acute performance benefits and long-term brain protection sets it apart from stimulant-based cognitive enhancers.

Semax is administered intranasally in Russian clinical practice, allowing direct delivery to the brain. In the research community, subcutaneous injection is also used. It remains a research compound without FDA approval in the United States.




## How It Works

To understand why Semax works, you need to understand BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor). BDNF is one of the most important proteins for brain health. It promotes the survival of existing neurons, encourages the growth of new neurons, and strengthens synaptic connections. Higher BDNF levels correlate with better learning, improved memory consolidation, and enhanced cognitive flexibility.

Semax increases BDNF expression in the hippocampus and cortex. Research shows that a single dose can increase BDNF protein levels by approximately 1.4-fold and BDNF mRNA expression by up to 3-fold. It also increases the phosphorylation of the TrkB receptor, which is how BDNF signals its effects inside neurons.

Beyond BDNF, Semax works through several other pathways:

- Increases serotonin metabolites by approximately 25% in the striatum
- Modulates dopamine release, particularly when combined with stimulants
- Provides neuroprotection under conditions of oxidative stress and hypoxia
- Affects the expression of genes related to immune response and vascular function
- May interact with melanocortin receptors (MC4 and MC5)

In stroke studies, genome-wide analysis showed that Semax affects 96 different genes at three hours post-dose, with over half related to immune response and vascular function. This broad gene-expression profile helps explain its neuroprotective effects during brain injury.

The practical result is enhanced focus, improved memory, better stress resilience, and protection against neurological damage. Unlike stimulants, which deplete neurotransmitters and lead to crashes, Semax builds up your brain's capacity through neurotrophic support.




## Benefits

**Cognitive Enhancement**

The primary benefit for healthy users is improved mental performance. Research shows enhanced learning, better memory formation, and sharper attention. Users commonly report heightened alertness, improved verbal fluency, and better working memory. These effects come from BDNF upregulation and neurotransmitter modulation rather than stimulation.

**Neuroprotection**

Semax protects neurons from damage caused by oxidative stress, hypoxia, and inflammation. In stroke models, it limits cell death and preserves brain function. This makes it valuable both for recovery from neurological injury and for long-term brain health maintenance.

**Stroke Recovery**

The strongest clinical evidence for Semax comes from stroke rehabilitation. Russian studies show that Semax treatment increases BDNF plasma levels, accelerates functional recovery, and improves motor performance in stroke patients. The effects are enhanced when combined with early rehabilitation.

**Mood and Stress Resilience**

Through its effects on serotonin and dopamine, Semax provides mood-stabilizing benefits. Users report improved emotional stability and better resilience under stress. This is not the same as anxiolysis (Selank is better for anxiety); it is more about maintaining cognitive performance when stressed.

**Optic Nerve Support**

In Russian clinical practice, Semax is used for optic nerve disorders. Studies show visual field expansion in 80% of treated eyes, with improvements in color vision and reduction of blind spots. The mechanism involves BDNF-mediated neuroprotection of the optic nerve.




## What the Science Shows

Semax has decades of clinical use in Russia and a substantial body of research, though most studies are from Russian journals.

**Dolotov et al. (2006), Brain Research**

This foundational study demonstrated that a single intranasal dose of Semax (50 mcg/kg) increased BDNF protein levels by 1.4-fold and BDNF mRNA by 3-fold in the rat hippocampus. TrkB receptor phosphorylation increased 1.6-fold. Semax-treated animals showed a distinct increase in conditioned avoidance reactions, supporting the cognitive enhancement mechanism.

**Gusev et al. (2018), Zhurnal Nevrologii i Psikhiatrii**

A clinical study of 110 stroke patients examined the effects of Semax on BDNF levels and functional recovery. Standard protocol: 6000 mcg/day for 10 days, repeated after a 20-day interval. Results showed that Semax administration increased BDNF plasma levels regardless of rehabilitation timing. High BDNF levels correlated with better Barthel scores (functional independence) and improved motor performance.

**Medvedeva et al. (2014), BMC Genomics**

Genome-wide analysis of Semax effects in rat brain focal ischemia (stroke model). The study found that Semax affected 96 genes at 3 hours post-administration, with over half related to immune response and vascular function. This helps explain the neuroprotective mechanism beyond simple BDNF upregulation.

**Eremin et al. (2005), Neurochemical Research**

This study showed that Semax activates both dopaminergic and serotonergic brain systems in rodents. Serotonin metabolites increased approximately 25% in the striatum. When combined with amphetamine, Semax potentiated dopamine release, suggesting synergy with stimulant pathways.

**Optic Nerve Studies**

Clinical studies in patients with glaucomatous optic neuropathy showed visual field expansion averaging 57.5 degrees in 80% of treated eyes after 30 days of Semax treatment. Patients also reported improved color perception and smaller blind spots.

# DSIP (Delta Sleep Inducing Peptide) 

## What It Is

DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide) is a naturally occurring neuropeptide first discovered in the 1970s by Swiss researchers studying sleep regulation. They isolated it from the blood of rabbits that had been induced into slow-wave (delta) sleep, which is how it got its name. DSIP is a small peptide consisting of only nine amino acids with the sequence Trp-Ala-Gly-Gly-Asp-Ala-Ser-Gly-Glu.

What makes DSIP different from typical sleep aids is that it does not sedate you. Instead of forcing drowsiness like benzodiazepines or antihistamines, DSIP appears to optimize the natural architecture of sleep. It promotes delta-wave sleep (the deepest, most restorative phase) while also influencing stress hormones, growth hormone release, and circadian rhythm regulation.

DSIP has been found in both free and bound forms in the hypothalamus, limbic system, pituitary, gut, and pancreas. It co-localizes with various hormones including ACTH, MSH, TSH, and glucagon. This wide distribution hints at its broad regulatory role beyond just sleep.

The peptide remains experimental. It has been studied in clinical trials for insomnia, chronic pain, alcohol and opioid withdrawal, and stress-related conditions, but it is not approved by any major regulatory agency. For people interested in improving sleep quality and recovery without sedation, DSIP offers a unique mechanism worth understanding.




## How It Works

Sleep is not simply "being unconscious." It is an active process with distinct stages, each serving different functions. Delta sleep (also called slow-wave sleep or Stage 3 NREM sleep) is the deepest phase. This is when physical restoration occurs: tissue repair, immune function, growth hormone release, and memory consolidation all happen primarily during delta sleep.

DSIP appears to promote this specific phase of sleep rather than just making you drowsy. The mechanism involves several pathways:

**GABA and NMDA Receptor Interaction**

Research suggests DSIP may interact with GABAergic systems (the same systems that benzodiazepines target) and NMDA receptors. However, unlike drugs that directly activate these receptors and cause sedation, DSIP seems to modulate the natural sleep-wake cycle without forcing sedation.

**Stress Hormone Regulation**

DSIP has been linked to the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, the system that controls cortisol and the stress response. Some research suggests DSIP can help normalize elevated cortisol levels, which is relevant for people whose sleep is disrupted by chronic stress.

**Growth Hormone and Pituitary Function**

DSIP may influence the release of growth hormone and luteinizing hormone from the pituitary. Growth hormone is released primarily during delta sleep, so enhancing deep sleep naturally supports GH secretion and recovery.

**Pain Modulation**

Early research suggested DSIP has analgesic properties, possibly through interaction with opioid systems. It has been studied for chronic pain conditions and withdrawal from opiates and alcohol.

The practical result is that DSIP users often report deeper, more continuous sleep without feeling drugged or groggy. They wake feeling more restored than they would from sedative sleep aids that suppress natural sleep architecture.




## Benefits

**Deeper, More Restorative Sleep**

The primary benefit is enhanced delta sleep. This is the phase of sleep most associated with physical recovery, immune function, and growth hormone release. Unlike sedatives that increase total sleep time but may actually reduce sleep quality, DSIP appears to improve the quality of sleep you get.

**Improved Sleep Continuity**

Users commonly report fewer awakenings during the night. Sleep becomes more continuous, which is important because fragmented sleep reduces the restorative benefits even if total sleep time is adequate.

**Stress Hormone Balance**

For people dealing with elevated cortisol from chronic stress, DSIP may help normalize the stress response. High cortisol at night disrupts sleep onset and sleep quality, creating a vicious cycle. By supporting HPA axis regulation, DSIP can help break this cycle.

**Growth Hormone Support**

Because GH is released primarily during delta sleep, improving deep sleep naturally supports growth hormone secretion. This benefits recovery, muscle repair, fat metabolism, and tissue healing. The effect is indirect (through better sleep) rather than direct GH stimulation.

**Recovery Enhancement**

Athletes and people recovering from intense training or injury may benefit from improved sleep quality. Sleep is when the body does most of its repair work, and delta sleep is the most important phase for physical recovery.

**Withdrawal Support**

Clinical research has explored DSIP for alcohol and opioid withdrawal. In one trial, 97% of opiate-dependent and 87% of alcohol-dependent patients reported symptom relief with DSIP administration. This is a specialized application but demonstrates the peptide's broader effects on stress and neural regulation.




## What the Science Shows

DSIP has been studied in humans, though the research is limited and much of it comes from European sources in the 1980s and 1990s.

**Schneider-Helmert et al. (1981), International Journal of Clinical Pharmacology**

This foundational study administered DSIP (25 nmol/kg) via slow intravenous infusion to six healthy volunteers. Subjects immediately reported a feeling of sleep pressure, and total sleep time increased by 59% within 130 minutes compared to placebo. Delayed effects on subsequent night sleep included shorter sleep onset, reduced Stage 1 sleep, and better sleep efficiency. The researchers noted that sophisticated analysis revealed no sedation in the classic pharmacological sense, suggesting DSIP sustains natural sleep functions rather than forcing them.

**Schneider-Helmert et al. (1992), Neuropsychobiology**

A double-blind study in 16 chronic insomnia patients examined DSIP (25 nmol/kg IV) administered over three consecutive afternoons. Results showed higher sleep efficiency and shorter sleep latency with DSIP compared to placebo. However, the researchers cautioned that effects were weak and suggested short-term treatment may not be of major therapeutic benefit for chronic insomnia.

**Larbig et al. (1984), European Neurology**

A pilot study examined DSIP in patients with chronic pronounced pain episodes. The researchers found that DSIP had effects on pain modulation, psychomotor performance, and concentration capacity. The study also referenced earlier work showing DSIP helped with withdrawal symptoms in alcoholics and opiate addicts.

**Lesch et al. (1988), Biological Psychiatry**

This study examined DSIP response to corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) in patients with major depressive disorder. It found that basal DSIP and cortisol concentrations were highly correlated and were higher in depressed patients than controls. The findings supported a modulatory function of DSIP in the HPA axis and its potential significance in stress-related conditions.

**Key Limitations**

The research on DSIP is limited by small sample sizes, varied methodologies, and age (most studies are from the 1980s and 1990s). The peptide has a very short half-life (approximately 15 minutes) due to rapid enzymatic degradation, which complicates dosing and may explain inconsistent results across studies.

# Pinealon 

## What It Is

Pinealon is a synthetic tripeptide made up of three amino acids: L-glutamic acid, L-aspartic acid, and L-arginine. The amino acid sequence is written as Glu-Asp-Arg, which is why you will also see Pinealon called "EDR peptide." It was originally isolated from Cortexin, a neuroprotective drug derived from pig brain tissue, and then synthesized in laboratory form.

Pinealon belongs to a class of compounds called peptide bioregulators. These are short chain peptides developed primarily in Russia that target specific organs or tissues. As the name suggests, Pinealon targets the pineal gland and nervous system.

What makes Pinealon unusual among peptides is its size. At just three amino acids, it is one of the smallest bioactive peptides studied for brain health. This tiny structure allows it to cross the blood brain barrier efficiently and even penetrate into cell nuclei where it may interact directly with DNA.

Pinealon is not FDA approved. It remains an experimental compound with most research coming from preclinical studies and limited human trials conducted primarily in Russia and Eastern Europe.

## How It Works

Pinealon operates through several mechanisms that distinguish it from larger peptides.

**Direct DNA Interaction**

Unlike most peptides that bind to cell surface receptors, Pinealon appears to cross cell membranes and enter the nucleus. Research suggests it can bind to specific DNA sequences and influence gene expression. This is called epigenetic regulation. It may turn certain genes on or off without changing the genetic code itself.

Molecular studies suggest Pinealon binds to the promoter region of the gene for 5-tryptophan hydroxylase, an enzyme critical for serotonin production. By enhancing this gene's activity, Pinealon may support serotonin synthesis in the brain.

**Antioxidant and Anti-Apoptotic Effects**

Research shows Pinealon increases the activity of protective enzymes including SOD2 (superoxide dismutase 2) and GPX1 (glutathione peroxidase 1). These enzymes neutralize reactive oxygen species that damage neurons.

Pinealon also suppresses caspase-3, an enzyme that executes cell death (apoptosis). By reducing caspase-3 activity, Pinealon may protect neurons from dying under stress conditions like oxygen deprivation.

**Neurotrophic Support**

Studies in brain cortex cell cultures show Pinealon boosts expression of 5-tryptophan hydroxylase, which increases serotonin production. Serotonin has known neuroprotective and geroprotective (anti-aging) properties.

**MAPK/ERK Pathway Modulation**

Pinealon affects cellular signaling cascades involved in cell survival and stress response. In neurons exposed to toxins, Pinealon delayed the activation of ERK1/2, which helps prevent premature cell death responses.

**Pineal Gland Effects**

The pineal gland produces melatonin, which regulates sleep-wake cycles. Preliminary research suggests Pinealon may help reset pineal gland function in conditions of circadian rhythm disruption, though this mechanism is not fully understood.

# Cerebrolysin 

## What It Is

Cerebrolysin is a mixture of low molecular weight peptides and free amino acids derived from porcine (pig) brain tissue. Unlike single peptides with a specific sequence, Cerebrolysin is a complex biological preparation containing over 100 different oligopeptides that work together. It is approximately 80% amino acids and 20% neuropeptides.

The peptides in Cerebrolysin include fragments that mimic the activity of natural neurotrophic factors: brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), nerve growth factor (NGF), glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), and ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF). These are the signaling molecules your brain uses to grow, repair, and maintain neurons.

Cerebrolysin was developed in Austria in 1949 and is approved for medical use in over 45 countries across Europe, Asia, and Russia. It is prescribed for stroke recovery, traumatic brain injury, dementia, and Alzheimer's disease. However, it is not FDA approved in the United States, and some large trials have questioned its benefits for acute stroke.

The small molecular weight of Cerebrolysin (under 10 kDa) allows it to cross the blood brain barrier, which is critical for any compound targeting the central nervous system. This distinguishes it from natural neurotrophic factors which cannot cross this barrier and must be administered invasively.

## How It Works

Cerebrolysin acts through multiple mechanisms that together provide broad neurotrophic and neuroprotective support.

**Neurotrophic Factor Mimicry**

The peptide fragments in Cerebrolysin mimic the effects of natural growth factors without binding to specific receptors. They interact with pathways associated with BDNF, NGF, GDNF, and CNTF, stimulating the same cellular processes these factors normally trigger. One clinical trial found Cerebrolysin increased serum BDNF levels by 300% over 16 weeks, and when combined with donepezil, BDNF increased by 600%.

**Neuroprotection**

Cerebrolysin protects neurons from multiple threats:

- Reduces excitotoxicity (damage from excessive glutamate stimulation)
- Inhibits free radical formation and oxidative stress
- Suppresses harmful microglial activation
- Blocks apoptosis (programmed cell death)
- Protects against lactic acidosis damage

These mechanisms are particularly relevant after stroke, traumatic brain injury, or during neurodegenerative disease progression.

**Neuroplasticity Enhancement**

Cerebrolysin supports the brain's ability to reorganize and form new connections:

- Increases synapse formation
- Promotes dendritic branching and spine density
- Supports axonal sprouting
- Enhances synaptic plasticity

**Neurogenesis Stimulation**

Research shows Cerebrolysin can stimulate the proliferation, differentiation, and migration of neural progenitor cells (stem cells) in the brain. This neurogenesis may contribute to recovery after injury and may slow decline in neurodegenerative conditions.

**Modulation of Key Signaling Pathways**

Cerebrolysin affects the neurotrophic factor (NTF) and sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling pathways. These regulate neurogenesis, angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation), myelination, and maintenance of the neurovascular unit.

**Amyloid Beta Effects**

In Alzheimer's disease models, Cerebrolysin decreased amyloid beta production by regulating the processing of amyloid precursor protein. It may also reduce existing amyloid deposits, though human evidence is limited.

## What the Science Shows

Cerebrolysin has extensive clinical research, though quality varies and results are mixed for some applications.

**Stroke**

The evidence for stroke is complicated. Cerebrolysin is approved for stroke treatment in many countries, but large trials have produced mixed results.

The CASTA trial (Cerebrolysin Acute Stroke Treatment in Asia) enrolled over 1,000 patients but failed to show benefit compared to placebo for composite stroke outcomes. A 2023 review concluded Cerebrolysin likely provides no benefit for preventing death in acute ischemic stroke and may increase the rate of adverse events requiring hospitalization.

However, when combined with rehabilitation therapy, results are more promising. A 2016 randomized controlled trial found that Cerebrolysin plus standardized rehabilitation produced better motor recovery and greater changes in the corticospinal tract compared to rehabilitation alone in patients with severe motor impairment.

**Alzheimer's Disease**

Evidence for Alzheimer's is more consistent. A meta-analysis of multiple trials found Cerebrolysin improved cognitive function in patients with mild to moderate Alzheimer's. Multiple 4-week treatment courses (5 days per week) improved cognition, with benefits maintained for up to 6 months after treatment ended.

A 24-week double blind placebo controlled study tested three dosages (10 mL, 30 mL, and 60 mL) in mild to moderate Alzheimer's patients. All doses showed benefit, with higher doses showing greater effects. Combining Cerebrolysin with donepezil (a standard Alzheimer's medication) produced synergistic increases in BDNF.

**Vascular Dementia**

A Cochrane review of six randomized trials found Cerebrolysin improved clinical symptoms compared to placebo. Benefits were observed for global function and cognitive measures, though the authors noted more high quality research is needed.

**Traumatic Brain Injury**

The CAPTAIN II trial studied Cerebrolysin in moderate to severe TBI and found improvements in neurorecovery. Multiple studies show benefits for cognitive and physical function after brain injury.

**Safety Data**

Cerebrolysin has an extensive safety track record from decades of clinical use. A systematic review of safety data from dementia and stroke trials confirmed it is generally well tolerated. Most adverse events are mild and temporary.

**Important Context**

The evidence base is mixed. For stroke, acute use has not proven beneficial in large trials. For dementia and TBI, smaller trials show promise. Much research comes from European and Asian investigators with financial ties to the manufacturer. Quality of evidence varies significantly across studies.
