# **Semaglutide (GLP-1 Agonist)** 

## What It Is

Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist that mimics the action of a naturally occurring gut hormone called glucagon-like peptide-1. It is FDA approved and available under multiple brand names: Wegovy for chronic weight management and Ozempic for type 2 diabetes.

Unlike retatrutide which is still investigational, semaglutide has been on the market since 2017 (Ozempic) and 2021 (Wegovy). It has extensive clinical trial data and real world use backing its safety and effectiveness.

Semaglutide is administered as a once weekly subcutaneous injection. The long half life of approximately 7 days allows for consistent blood levels with weekly dosing.




## How It Works

Semaglutide activates GLP-1 receptors throughout the body, triggering several effects that support weight loss and metabolic health.

**Appetite Suppression** GLP-1 receptors in the brain (hypothalamus and brainstem) regulate hunger signals. Semaglutide reduces appetite and increases feelings of fullness, making it easier to eat less without feeling deprived.

**Slowed Gastric Emptying** Food stays in the stomach longer, which prolongs satiety after meals and reduces the urge to snack between meals.

**Improved Insulin Response** Semaglutide enhances insulin secretion when blood sugar is elevated and reduces glucagon release. This improves blood sugar control without causing hypoglycemia in most users.

**Reduced Food Reward** Research suggests GLP-1 agonists reduce the reward value of food, making high calorie foods less appealing and reducing cravings.




## Benefits

**Weight Loss** Clinical trials (STEP-1 and STEP-3) showed participants taking 2.4 mg weekly lost an average of 15% of their body weight over 68 weeks. This is significantly more than older weight loss medications.

**Blood Sugar Control** Even in people without diabetes, semaglutide improves fasting glucose and insulin sensitivity. For those with type 2 diabetes, it produces meaningful reductions in HbA1c.

**Cardiovascular Protection** The SELECT trial demonstrated that semaglutide reduces the risk of major cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke, cardiovascular death) by 20% in people with obesity and established cardiovascular disease.

**Sustained Results** Unlike crash diets, semaglutide produces steady weight loss over months. Most users continue losing weight through the full titration period and into maintenance.




## What the Science Shows

Large clinical trials have established semaglutide as one of the most effective weight loss medications available.

**STEP-1 Trial (2021)** Published in the New England Journal of Medicine. 1,961 adults with obesity received either semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo for 68 weeks. Results:

- Semaglutide group: 14.9% average weight loss
- Placebo group: 2.4% average weight loss
- 86% of semaglutide users lost at least 5% of body weight
- 69% lost at least 10%
- 50% lost at least 15%

**STEP-3 Trial** Combined semaglutide with intensive behavioral therapy. Participants lost an average of 16% of body weight, showing that lifestyle changes amplify the medication's effects.

**SELECT Trial (2023)** Cardiovascular outcomes trial with over 17,000 participants. Semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 20% compared to placebo in people with obesity and heart disease.

## Dosing Protocol

Semaglutide dosing depends on your goal and how your body responds. Understanding why different doses exist will help you find the right level for your situation.

**Understanding the Dose Range**

You will see semaglutide doses ranging from 0.25 mg to 2.4 mg weekly. This range exists because the medication was developed for different purposes and different populations respond to different doses.

**Diabetic Management vs Weight Loss**

Semaglutide was originally developed for type 2 diabetes under the brand name Ozempic, with doses up to 1.0 to 2.0 mg weekly. The goal was blood sugar control, and these doses effectively improve insulin sensitivity and lower A1c.

When researchers studied higher doses for weight loss (Wegovy), they found that 2.4 mg weekly produced significantly more weight loss than the diabetes doses. The STEP-1 trial showed nearly 15% average weight loss at 2.4 mg compared to what lower doses achieve.

**Not Everyone Needs the Maximum Dose**

The 2.4 mg target is based on clinical trial protocols designed to show maximum effect. In practice, many people achieve excellent results at lower doses.

Some people lose significant weight at 1.0 mg and experience minimal side effects. Pushing to 2.4 mg may add more side effects without proportional benefit. Others need the full 2.4 mg to see meaningful results.

Your optimal dose depends on:

- How much weight you need to lose
- How sensitive you are to the appetite suppression effects
- How well you tolerate the gastrointestinal side effects
- Whether you are using it for metabolic health vs significant weight loss

**Matching Dose to Goal**

Metabolic optimization and modest weight loss (5 to 10%): Many people achieve this at 0.5 to 1.0 mg weekly with good tolerance. This is appropriate if you want metabolic benefits without aggressive weight loss.

Significant weight loss (10 to 15%+): The clinical trial data supports 2.4 mg weekly for maximum weight loss effect. Be prepared for more significant side effects during titration.

Blood sugar control (type 2 diabetes): 0.5 to 2.0 mg weekly is the therapeutic range, titrated based on A1c response.

**Standard Titration Protocol**

Semaglutide follows a slow titration to reduce gastrointestinal side effects. The dose increases every 4 weeks until reaching the maintenance dose.

**Subcutaneous injection once weekly.**

- Weeks 1 to 4: 0.25 mg (starting dose to allow adaptation)
- Weeks 5 to 8: 0.5 mg (first increase, some weight loss begins)
- Weeks 9 to 12: 1.0 mg (therapeutic effects strengthen)
- Weeks 13 to 16: 1.7 mg (approaching maintenance)
- Week 17 and onward: 2.4 mg (full maintenance dose for weight loss)

**For Type 2 Diabetes (Ozempic):** The maximum dose is typically 1.0 to 2.0 mg weekly, lower than the weight loss protocol.

**Important Notes:**

- Do not skip titration steps. Starting at higher doses causes severe nausea and vomiting.
- If side effects are strong at any step, stay at that dose for an additional 4 weeks before increasing.
- Some people achieve good results at 1.0 or 1.7 mg without needing the full 2.4 mg dose.
- Take your injection on the same day each week. You can change the day as long as your last dose was at least 2 days prior.

## Side Effects and Cautions

**Common Effects (especially during titration):**

- Nausea (most common, affects up to 44% of users initially)
- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Constipation
- Abdominal pain
- Headache
- Fatigue

These effects are dose dependent and typically improve after the first 4 to 8 weeks as your body adapts. Slow titration significantly reduces severity.

**Less Common:**

- Acid reflux or heartburn
- Bloating and gas
- Dizziness
- Injection site reactions

**When to Contact a Medical Professional:**

- Persistent vomiting that does not resolve
- Severe abdominal pain (possible pancreatitis)
- Signs of allergic reaction (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing)
- Signs of thyroid tumors (neck mass, difficulty swallowing, persistent hoarseness)
- Severe hypoglycemia if taking with insulin or sulfonylureas




## Who Should Avoid or Use With Care

**Do not use if you have:**

- Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
- Multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2)
- Known hypersensitivity to semaglutide
- Current pregnancy or breastfeeding

**Use with caution and medical supervision if you have:**

- History of pancreatitis
- Diabetic retinopathy
- Kidney disease
- History of gallbladder disease
- Depression or history of suicidal thoughts
- Gastroparesis or other GI motility disorders

**Drug Interactions:**

- If taking insulin or sulfonylureas, doses may need to be reduced to prevent hypoglycemia
- Semaglutide slows gastric emptying, which can affect absorption of oral medications

Consult a qualified medical provider before use if any of these apply.

## Injection Technique

1. Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water
2. Clean the vial stopper with an alcohol swab and allow to air dry
3. Draw the appropriate dose into a sterile insulin syringe
4. Clean the injection site with an alcohol swab
5. Pinch a skinfold and insert the needle at 45 to 90 degrees into subcutaneous tissue
6. Do not aspirate for subcutaneous injections
7. Inject slowly and steadily
8. Withdraw needle and apply light pressure with gauze if needed
9. Dispose of syringe immediately in a sharps container. Never recap needles.
10. Rotate injection sites weekly (abdomen, thighs, upper arms) at least 1 inch apart

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How fast will I lose weight?** Most people lose 1 to 2 pounds per week once they reach therapeutic doses. Significant results typically appear by week 12 to 16. Full effects develop over 6 to 12 months.

**How does it compare to tirzepatide?** Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) produces about 6% more weight loss on average due to dual receptor activation. Semaglutide has more long term safety data since it has been on the market longer.

**Can I drink alcohol?** Moderate alcohol is generally tolerated but can worsen nausea and may affect blood sugar. Start with small amounts to see how you respond.

**Will I regain weight if I stop?** Studies show most people regain weight after stopping semaglutide. Long term or indefinite use may be necessary to maintain results. Lifestyle changes during treatment can help reduce regain.

**Can I take it with other medications?** Semaglutide can affect absorption of oral medications due to slowed gastric emptying. Discuss all medications with your provider. Do not combine with other GLP-1 agonists.

**Is nausea unavoidable?** Most people experience some nausea, but slow titration significantly reduces severity. It typically improves after the first month. Eating smaller meals and avoiding fatty foods helps.

**Do I need to inject in my stomach?** No. You can inject in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate sites to prevent irritation.

# Tirzepatide (Dual GIP & GLP-1 Agonist)

## What It Is

Tirzepatide is a dual receptor agonist that changed what people thought was possible with weight loss drugs. It activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors at the same time, and this combination produces significantly better results than GLP-1 drugs alone.

In the SURMOUNT trials, tirzepatide produced an average weight loss of over 20%, with many participants losing 25% or more of their body weight. To put that in perspective, if you weigh 250 pounds, that is 50 to 60 pounds lost. These numbers were previously only achievable through bariatric surgery.

Tirzepatide is FDA approved under two brand names: Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity. It is manufactured by Eli Lilly and administered as a once weekly subcutaneous injection. Research grade tirzepatide is also available through peptide suppliers.




## How It Works

To understand why tirzepatide outperforms semaglutide, you need to understand what each receptor does.

**GLP-1 Receptor**

This is the same receptor that semaglutide targets. When activated, it reduces appetite, slows gastric emptying, and improves insulin sensitivity. You feel full faster and stay full longer. This pathway is well established and has been the foundation of obesity drugs for years.

**GIP Receptor**

This is what makes tirzepatide different. GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) is another hormone involved in metabolism. For a long time, researchers thought blocking GIP would help with weight loss. It turns out they had it backwards. Activating GIP alongside GLP-1 produces better results than GLP-1 alone.

Tirzepatide is actually about 5 times more potent at the GIP receptor than your body's natural GIP hormone. This enhanced GIP activity appears to improve how your body processes fat and may contribute to better tolerability compared to pure GLP-1 drugs.

**The Combined Effect**

When both receptors are activated together, you get stronger appetite suppression than either pathway alone. Your body handles blood sugar more efficiently. And there is emerging evidence that the dual mechanism helps preserve more lean mass during weight loss compared to GLP-1 only drugs.

The half life is approximately 5 days, which supports stable blood levels with once weekly dosing.




## Benefits

**Superior Weight Loss**

The SURMOUNT-1 trial showed an average weight loss of 22.5% at the 15 mg dose over 72 weeks. More than a third of participants lost 25% or more of their body weight. These results beat semaglutide head to head in the SURMOUNT-5 trial.

**Better Than Semaglutide Head to Head**

SURMOUNT-5 directly compared tirzepatide to semaglutide in people without diabetes. Tirzepatide at 15 mg produced 20.2% weight loss versus 13.7% for semaglutide 2.4 mg over 72 weeks. That is a meaningful difference.

**Powerful Blood Sugar Control**

In the SURPASS trials for type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide reduced HbA1c by up to 2.3%. Some participants achieved normal blood sugar levels without diabetes medication. Weight loss in these trials ranged from 15 to 25 pounds depending on dose.

**Testosterone Restoration in Men**

Recent data presented at ENDO 2025 showed that tirzepatide increased total testosterone in men with obesity. This makes sense because excess body fat converts testosterone to estrogen. Lose the fat, restore the hormone levels. Men saw improvements in sexual function and quality of life alongside weight loss.

**Cardiovascular Benefits**

The SURMOUNT-OSA trial showed tirzepatide reduced sleep apnea severity by up to 62.8% and improved blood pressure. Similar cardiovascular benefits are expected based on the metabolic improvements.

**Once Weekly Dosing**

You inject once per week. Consistency is easy to maintain.




## What the Science Shows

Tirzepatide has extensive clinical trial data across the SURMOUNT (obesity) and SURPASS (diabetes) programs.

**SURMOUNT-1 Trial (2022)**

Published in the New England Journal of Medicine. 2,539 adults with obesity (without diabetes) received tirzepatide or placebo for 72 weeks.

Results by dose:

## Dosing Protocol

Tirzepatide dosing follows a titration schedule, but your optimal maintenance dose depends on your response and goals. Understanding the dose-response relationship will help you find the right level.

**Understanding the Dose Range**

Clinical trials tested tirzepatide at 5 mg, 10 mg, and 15 mg weekly. The weight loss results were dose-dependent:

- 5 mg weekly: 15% average weight loss
- 10 mg weekly: 19.5% average weight loss
- 15 mg weekly: 22.5% average weight loss

This shows a clear pattern: higher doses produce more weight loss, but with diminishing returns. The jump from 5 mg to 10 mg added 4.5 percentage points of weight loss. The jump from 10 mg to 15 mg added only 3 more percentage points.

**Not Everyone Needs Maximum Dose**

The 15 mg maximum dose was designed to show maximum efficacy in clinical trials. But the trial data also shows that 10 mg produces nearly 20% weight loss on average, which is more than most people need.

Consider your actual goal:

If you need to lose 15% of body weight, the 5 mg dose may get you there with minimal side effects.

If you need to lose 20% or more, you may benefit from pushing to 10 mg or 15 mg.

If you are using tirzepatide primarily for metabolic health (blood sugar, insulin sensitivity) rather than aggressive weight loss, lower doses may be sufficient.

**Matching Dose to Response**

Start with the standard titration. At each dose level, assess:

- Are you losing weight at a satisfactory rate?
- Are side effects manageable?
- Is your appetite appropriately suppressed?

If you are losing 1 to 2 pounds per week at 7.5 mg or 10 mg with tolerable side effects, you may not need to increase further. The goal is finding the minimum effective dose that achieves your results, not automatically pushing to maximum.

**Standard Titration Protocol**

Tirzepatide follows a gradual titration schedule to minimize side effects. The protocol below matches the FDA approved labeling and clinical trial design.

**Subcutaneous injection once weekly.**

- Weeks 1 to 4: 2.5 mg
- Weeks 5 to 8: 5 mg
- Weeks 9 to 12: 7.5 mg
- Weeks 13 to 16: 10 mg
- Weeks 17 to 20: 12.5 mg
- Week 21 and onward: 15 mg (maximum dose)

**Important Notes:**

Many people get excellent results at 10 mg without ever needing 15 mg. If you are losing weight steadily and tolerating your current dose well, there is no rule that says you must push to maximum.

The 2.5 mg starting dose is intentionally low to let your body adapt. Do not skip it.

If side effects are rough at any step, stay at that dose for another 4 weeks before increasing. The trial data showed that slower titration improved tolerability without sacrificing results.

Take your injection on the same day each week. Consistency matters for stable blood levels.

## Side Effects and Cautions

The side effects are similar to other GLP-1 drugs. The dual mechanism does not appear to increase side effect severity compared to semaglutide, and some data suggests it may actually be better tolerated.

**Common Effects:**

- Nausea (most common, affects 25 to 30% depending on dose)
- Diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Constipation
- Decreased appetite
- Abdominal discomfort

These are dose dependent and typically improve after the first few weeks at each dose level. Most people find them manageable with proper titration.

**Less Common:**

- Injection site reactions
- Fatigue
- Hair thinning (related to rapid weight loss, not the drug itself)
- Heartburn or acid reflux

**When to Contact a Medical Professional:**

- Persistent vomiting that will not resolve
- Severe abdominal pain (could indicate pancreatitis or gallbladder issues)
- Signs of allergic reaction (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing)
- Vision changes (especially if you have diabetic retinopathy)

## Success Tips

The most common mistake is moving up too fast. Your body needs time to adapt to the new signals. If a dose is rough, stay there longer before increasing.

**Keep Training**

Walk daily. Lift weights 2 to 3 times per week. The weight loss will happen either way, but resistance training ensures more of what you lose is fat and less is muscle. Do not rely on the drug alone.

**Track Your Progress**

Weigh yourself weekly at the same time. Take measurements monthly. Progress photos help. Weight was still declining at week 72 in the trials, so patience pays off.

# Retatrutide (GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon Triple Agonist) 

## What It Is

Retatrutide is the most powerful weight loss peptide currently in clinical trials. It works by activating three different receptor systems at once: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. This triple action is why it produces better results than semaglutide or tirzepatide in head to head comparisons.

To put this in perspective, semaglutide hits one receptor and produces around 15% weight loss. Tirzepatide hits two receptors and produces around 21% weight loss. Retatrutide hits three receptors and in Phase 2 trials, participants lost an average of 24% of their body weight at the highest dose.

The compound was developed by Eli Lilly and is currently in Phase 3 trials. It is not yet FDA approved, but it is available through research peptide suppliers. Like the other incretin drugs, it is administered as a once weekly subcutaneous injection.




## How It Works

To understand why retatrutide works so well, you need to understand what each receptor does.

**GLP-1 Receptor**

This is the same receptor that semaglutide targets. When activated, it reduces your appetite, slows down how fast food leaves your stomach, and improves how your body handles insulin. You feel full faster and stay full longer.

**GIP Receptor**

This receptor enhances insulin release and appears to improve how your body processes fat. Retatrutide is actually about 9 times more potent at this receptor than your body's natural GIP hormone. Tirzepatide also hits this receptor, which is part of why it outperforms semaglutide.

**Glucagon Receptor**

This is what sets retatrutide apart. Glucagon tells your body to release stored energy. When this receptor is activated, your body increases energy expenditure and ramps up fat burning in the liver. You are not just eating less, you are actually burning more calories.

The combination of all three means you eat less, digest slower, handle blood sugar better, and burn more energy. That is why the weight loss numbers are so much higher than single or dual receptor drugs.




## Benefits

**Highest Weight Loss in Any Drug Trial**

At the 12 mg dose, participants in the Phase 2 trial lost an average of 24.2% of their body weight over 48 weeks. To put that in real numbers, if you weigh 250 pounds, that is about 60 pounds lost in under a year.

**Nearly Everyone Responds**

At the 8 mg and 12 mg doses, 100% of trial participants achieved at least 5% weight loss. At 12 mg, 93% lost at least 10% and 83% lost at least 15%. These response rates are higher than any other obesity drug.

**Metabolic Health Improvements**

In trials with type 2 diabetics, retatrutide reduced HbA1c by up to 2.0% while also producing significant weight loss. Blood pressure, triglycerides, and cholesterol markers also improved.

**Increased Calorie Burn**

Because of the glucagon receptor activation, retatrutide does not rely solely on appetite suppression. Your body actually burns more calories at rest. This may help with long term weight maintenance.

**Once Weekly Dosing**

The half life is approximately 6 days, so you only need to inject once per week.




## What the Science Shows

Retatrutide has solid clinical trial data from Phase 1, 2, and ongoing Phase 3 studies.

**Phase 2 Obesity Trial (2023)**

This is the landmark study published in the New England Journal of Medicine. 338 adults with obesity received retatrutide at various doses or placebo for 48 weeks.

Results by dose:

- 1 mg weekly: 8.7% average weight loss
- 4 mg weekly: 17.1% average weight loss
- 8 mg weekly: 22.8% average weight loss
- 12 mg weekly: 24.2% average weight loss
- Placebo: 2.1% average weight loss

At the 12 mg dose, about 63% of participants lost at least 20% of their body weight, and roughly 25% lost 30% or more. Weight was still declining at week 48, which suggests longer treatment would produce even greater results.

**Phase 2 Type 2 Diabetes Trial (2023)**

Published in The Lancet. Participants with type 2 diabetes achieved approximately 17% weight loss at 36 weeks alongside major improvements in blood sugar control.

**Starting Dose Matters**

The trial tested starting at 2 mg versus 4 mg. Starting lower and escalating more slowly resulted in significantly fewer side effects with similar weight loss outcomes at 48 weeks. This is why the recommended protocol starts at 2 mg.

# Cagrilintide (Amylin Analog, DACRA) 

## Cagrilintide




## What It Is

Cagrilintide is a long acting amylin analog that works through a completely different pathway than GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide, tirzepatide, and retatrutide. While those drugs have dominated the weight loss conversation, cagrilintide offers something none of them can: appetite suppression through a second, independent system in your brain that GLP-1 compounds do not touch.

Amylin is a hormone your pancreas releases alongside insulin every time you eat. Its job is to tell your brain you are full. The problem is that natural amylin breaks down in your body within minutes. It does not last long enough to make a sustained impact. Cagrilintide is engineered to last 7 to 8 days, which is why you only need to inject once per week. Think of it as a louder, cleaner version of a signal your body already produces.

The real power of cagrilintide is in combination therapy. In the REDEFINE 1 trial, cagrilintide paired with semaglutide (called CagriSema) produced over 20% average weight loss, with 60% of participants losing 20% or more of their body weight and 23% losing 30% or more. Those are among the most significant results seen with any anti-obesity medication to date.

While the clinical trials studied cagrilintide combined with semaglutide, the mechanism applies to any GLP-1 type compound. Pairing cagrilintide with retatrutide, tirzepatide, or semaglutide adds a full strength appetite signal through the amylin pathway that none of those compounds activate on their own. This makes cagrilintide a primary strategy for anyone using a GLP-1 type peptide who still experiences significant hunger or food noise.

Cagrilintide is currently in Phase 3 clinical trials. It is not yet FDA approved but is available through research peptide suppliers.




## How It Works

To understand why cagrilintide is so effective as an add-on to GLP-1 compounds, you need to understand that appetite is controlled by two separate systems in your brain.

**Two Different Brain Regions, Two Different Jobs**

GLP-1 works in the hypothalamus. This is the part of your brain that manages your baseline drive to seek food. Think of it as your appetite thermostat. It controls how much you think about food throughout the day, how often you want to eat, and how strong your cravings are.

Amylin works in the brainstem, specifically an area called the area postrema. This is the part of your brain that manages immediate gut signals. It detects what is happening in your stomach right now and tells you when to stop eating. Think of it as your satiety switch.

The simple way to understand the difference: the hypothalamus controls how much you want to start eating. The brainstem controls when to stop once you have started.

The neurons that respond to amylin do not even have GLP-1 receptors on them. They are completely separate systems. So if you have been on semaglutide for a year and those GLP-1 receptors are experiencing some degree of tolerance, your amylin receptors have not been touched by those drugs at all.

**The Amylin Resistance Problem**

As metabolic health declines and insulin resistance develops, your body starts pumping out more insulin trying to compensate. Because amylin gets released alongside insulin, more insulin means more amylin. When amylin stays elevated all the time, your receptors start to downregulate. They stop responding as strongly.

This creates a vicious cycle. The worse your metabolic health gets, the harder it becomes for your body to tell you to stop eating. Your natural satiety system breaks down, which makes you eat more, which worsens insulin resistance, which pumps out more insulin and amylin, which further downregulates the receptors. This is exactly why it feels impossible to stop eating once you start for many people dealing with metabolic dysfunction. It is not just willpower. There is an actual physiological breakdown happening.

Cagrilintide cuts through this resistance because it is stronger and lasts longer than what your body produces naturally. Your worn out receptors can still respond to it because the signal is so much stronger than the weak, pulsatile natural amylin they have been ignoring.

**Slowed Gastric Emptying**

Like GLP-1 drugs, cagrilintide slows down how fast food leaves your stomach. This means meals keep you satisfied longer.

**Why Combination Works**

When you combine cagrilintide with a GLP-1 compound, your brain gets the "stop eating" message through two different channels instead of one. GLP-1 turns down the thermostat so you are not thinking about food as much between meals. Amylin flips the satiety switch faster so you put the fork down sooner during meals. Two different parts of the brain, two different jobs. That is why the combination works better than either one alone.

**The Half Life Advantage**

Cagrilintide has a half life of about 7 to 8 days, which supports stable blood levels with once weekly dosing.




## Why This Matters for Retatrutide Users

One of the biggest problems with switching from semaglutide or tirzepatide to retatrutide is the appetite suppression is not nearly as strong. People switch, the food noise comes back, and they get frustrated.

The reason is that retatrutide was designed to activate three receptors: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. When you engineer one molecule to hit three different targets, you cannot maximize all of them. Retatrutide is about 9 times more potent at the GIP receptor but only about 40% as potent at GLP-1 compared to semaglutide. GLP-1 is the receptor primarily responsible for appetite suppression. That is why hunger feels stronger on retatrutide.

The tradeoff was intentional. The glucagon activation is what makes retatrutide so powerful for fat loss. Glucagon tells your liver to burn stored fat. Your liver becomes a furnace constantly burning calories even at rest. But you cannot feel that happening. Hunger is a sensation you experience. Liver fat oxidation is not.

This is exactly why adding cagrilintide to retatrutide makes mechanical sense. Retatrutide does not touch amylin receptors at all. When you add cagrilintide, you are adding a full strength appetite signal through a completely different system. You are not doubling up on the same pathway like you would be if you stacked semaglutide with retatrutide. You are adding a new one.

Stacking cagrilintide with retatrutide has not been studied in clinical trials. The researched combination is cagrilintide plus semaglutide. The retatrutide pairing is based on the mechanism of action and significant anecdotal evidence from the community, not published clinical data. That said, the pharmacology supports it. Amylin is completely separate from GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon. There is no receptor overlap.

# AOD-9604 (HGH Fragment 176-191) 

## What It Is

AOD-9604 is a modified fragment of human growth hormone. Specifically, it is derived from amino acids 176 through 191 from the tail end of the HGH molecule, with one structural modification. Researchers isolated this piece because early studies suggested it was responsible for the fat burning effects of growth hormone without the other effects like raising blood sugar or IGF-1.

The idea was appealing. Growth hormone helps burn fat, but it also causes side effects like insulin resistance, water retention, and potential tumor growth concerns. If you could isolate just the fat burning part, you would have the benefit without the downsides.

AOD-9604 went through extensive human clinical trials in the early 2000s. It completed six trials with over 900 participants. The compound was safe, but the weight loss results were inconsistent. Development was terminated in 2007 after a large Phase 2b trial failed to show statistical significance over placebo.

So why are people still interested in it? The safety profile is excellent, the cost is low, and some users report good results even if the clinical trial data was underwhelming. It is available through research peptide suppliers.




## AOD-9604 vs HGH Fragment 176-191 

These two names get used interchangeably in the research peptide market, but they are technically different molecules. Understanding the distinction matters because only one of them has human clinical data behind it.

HGH Fragment 176-191 is the last 16 amino acids of human growth hormone, cut directly from the native molecule with no modifications. The amino acid at the front of that fragment (position 176) is phenylalanine, exactly as it appears in full length HGH. This unmodified fragment has never been studied in humans. All claims about its fat burning properties are extrapolated from AOD-9604 data, which is a different compound. (Wikipedia. "HGH Fragment 176-191." [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HGH_Fragment_176-191](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HGH_Fragment_176-191 "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HGH_Fragment_176-191"))

AOD-9604 is the same 176-191 fragment with one modification: the phenylalanine at the N-terminal end is replaced with tyrosine. Tyrosine is structurally almost identical to phenylalanine. The only difference is an additional hydroxyl (OH) group on the aromatic ring. That single amino acid swap gives AOD-9604 improved stability and allowed researchers to track it more precisely during clinical trials. AOD stands for "Anti-Obesity Drug," reflecting the compound's original development purpose by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals in Australia during the 1990s. (Wikipedia. "AOD9604." [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOD9604](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOD9604 "https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AOD9604"))

Why this matters: AOD-9604 is the version that completed six human clinical trials with over 900 participants. It is the version with established safety data. It is the version that received FDA GRAS status. HGH Fragment 176-191, the unmodified version, has zero human data. When you see research peptide suppliers selling either name, you should know that the clinical evidence only applies to AOD-9604.

One other important structural distinction: AOD-9604 does not bind the human growth hormone receptor. The Heffernan 2001 study confirmed this using cells transfected with the HGH receptor. AOD-9604 did not compete for receptor binding and did not induce cell proliferation through the HGH receptor. This is why it does not raise IGF-1, does not affect blood sugar, and does not produce the anabolic or growth promoting effects of full length HGH. It works through a completely separate pathway. (Heffernan M, et al. "The effects of human GH and its lipolytic fragment (AOD9604) on lipid metabolism following chronic treatment in obese mice and beta(3)-AR knock-out mice." Endocrinology. 2001;142(12):5182-5189. PMID: 11713213)




## How It Works 

AOD-9604 works through beta-3 adrenergic receptor activation, which stimulates lipolysis (the breakdown of stored fat into fatty acids that your body can burn for energy).

The Heffernan 2001 study demonstrated this mechanism using beta-3 adrenergic receptor knockout mice. Both normal mice and knockout mice were treated with AOD-9604 for 14 days. Normal mice showed reduced body weight gain and increased lipolysis. The knockout mice did not respond, confirming that beta-3 adrenergic receptors are required for AOD-9604's chronic lipolytic effects. The study also showed that both HGH and AOD-9604 increased the expression of beta-3 adrenergic receptor RNA in obese mice to levels comparable with lean mice, suggesting the compound may restore normal fat metabolism signaling. (Heffernan M, et al. Endocrinology. 2001;142(12):5182-5189. PMID: 11713213)

What It Does:

Stimulates fat breakdown in adipose tissue

May increase fat oxidation (burning fat for fuel)

Does not affect growth hormone receptors

Does not increase IGF-1 levels

Does not affect blood sugar or insulin

What It Does Not Do:

This is important to understand. AOD-9604 is not growth hormone. It does not provide the anabolic effects of HGH. It will not build muscle, improve sleep quality, enhance recovery, or provide anti-aging benefits. It is specifically the lipolytic (fat burning) fragment and nothing more.

The mechanism is also different from the incretin drugs like semaglutide or tirzepatide. Those work by reducing appetite and slowing digestion. AOD-9604 does not significantly affect hunger. It works on the fat cells themselves.

Half Life:

## What the Science Shows 

AOD-9604 has more human clinical trial data than most research peptides. The results were mixed.

Phase 2a Trial (2001)

54 obese subjects received AOD-9604 or placebo for 12 weeks. The treatment group lost an average of 2.6 kg (about 5.7 pounds) compared to 0.8 kg in the placebo group. This was statistically significant and encouraged further development.

Phase 2b Trial (2004)

This was the larger trial that ultimately ended development. 536 subjects received various doses of AOD-9604 or placebo for 24 weeks. The primary endpoint (weight loss at 24 weeks) did not achieve statistical significance versus placebo.

Some doses showed trends toward benefit, but the results were inconsistent across dose groups. The sponsor concluded the compound did not have sufficient efficacy to justify continued development.

Metabolic Studies

Smaller studies confirmed that AOD-9604 does not affect glucose, insulin, or IGF-1 levels. The safety profile was consistently favorable.

Safety and Toxicology

Extensive safety testing included an Ames test for mutagenic potential, a chromosomal aberration assay, a bone micronucleus assay, a 6-month chronic toxicology study in rats, and a 9-month chronic toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys. AOD-9604 showed no genotoxic, mutagenic, or significant toxic effects across all studies. (Stier H, et al. "Safety and Tolerability of the Hexadecapeptide AOD9604 in Humans." Journal of Endocrinology and Metabolism. 2013;3(1-2):7-15)

Cartilage Research

Interestingly, AOD-9604 has shown some promise for cartilage repair and osteoarthritis in preclinical studies. This is a different application than weight loss and is still being explored.

The Bottom Line

The clinical trial data for weight loss is weaker than what we see with GLP-1 drugs. AOD-9604 did not demonstrate consistent, significant weight loss in large trials. This does not mean it does nothing, but expectations should be calibrated accordingly.




## Reconstitution 

AOD-9604 is structurally different from other peptides and requires different handling during reconstitution.

Why AOD-9604 Gels

AOD is a chain of 16 amino acids. About six of those are hydrophobic. When you add water to the vial, those amino acids drive the molecules to cluster together to get away from the liquid. That clustering is what starts gel formation. This is called aggregation.

AOD also has two cysteine amino acids connected by a sulfur bridge called a disulfide bond that holds the peptide in a loop shape. That bridge can break, and when it does the exposed sulfur on one molecule bonds to the exposed sulfur on another. This creates chains that grow into the visible clumps you see in your vial.

Every time you agitate or shake the vial, you create bubbles. Those bubbles create surfaces where air meets liquid called air to liquid interfaces. The hydrophobic parts of the molecule orient toward the air, which pulls the molecule apart and exposes parts that should stay protected. When those bubbles collapse, the exposed molecules stick together. All three of these mechanisms feed into each other, which is why AOD gels more aggressively than anything else you have worked with.

Why Acetic Acid Works

Do not use bacteriostatic water by itself for AOD-9604. Use 0.6% acetic acid solution instead.

Acetic acid works for two specific reasons. First, AOD carries a positive electrical charge and acidic solutions make that charge stronger. Since molecules with the same charge repel each other, the acid keeps the molecules separated instead of clustering. Second, the acid slows down the sulfur bonding, preventing molecules from chaining together when the disulfide bonds break.

Preferred Method

Add 2 ml of 0.6% acetic acid solution slowly down the inside wall of the vial. Do not spray directly on the powder. Do not shake. Let the powder dissolve on its own. This may take several minutes. Swirl gently if needed.

Alternative Method

Use 1 ml of 0.6% acetic acid and 1 ml of bacteriostatic water for a 50/50 mix. The acetic acid provides enough pH reduction for stability while the bacteriostatic water provides the benzyl alcohol preservative. Add the acetic acid first, let the powder begin dissolving, then add the bacteriostatic water slowly down the wall.

After reconstitution, consider preloading your syringes. This way you only disturb the solution once instead of piercing the stopper and drawing from it repeatedly, which introduces agitation each time.

Store reconstituted AOD-9604 in the refrigerator at 2 to 8C. Use within 28 days.

What Is Normal vs What Is Not

Normal: AOD will eventually gel over time even with perfect technique. Some sources claim gelled AOD is still functional.

Not normal: A glob that never dissolves from day one combined with the solution turning thick and milky means something went wrong. This could be degradation before it reached you, improper manufacturing, or contamination during reconstitution. Do not use a vial that looks like this. Contact the supplier for a replacement.

# Adipotide (Fat Targeted Proapoptotic Peptide) 

## What It Is

Adipotide is a peptide that kills fat cells by cutting off their blood supply. Unlike most weight loss compounds that work by reducing appetite or increasing metabolism, adipotide targets the blood vessels that feed white adipose tissue and causes those vessels to die. When the blood supply is cut, the fat cells starve and undergo apoptosis (programmed cell death).

The compound is also known as FTPP (Fat Targeted Proapoptotic Peptide) and Prohibitin-TP01. It was originally developed for cancer treatment with the goal of starving tumors by destroying their blood supply. Researchers noticed dramatic fat loss in animal models and began exploring it for obesity.

The preclinical results were remarkable. Obese mice lost around 30% of their body weight in 4 weeks. Obese rhesus monkeys lost up to 39% of their body weight with improved metabolic markers. A Phase 1 human trial started in 2011 but clinical development appears to have been discontinued as of 2019.

Adipotide works through a fundamentally different mechanism than any other weight loss compound. This makes it scientifically interesting but also raises unique safety considerations. It is available through research peptide suppliers, though human data is extremely limited.




## How It Works

Adipotide has two functional parts: a targeting sequence and a cell killing sequence.

**The Targeting Sequence**

The first part of the peptide binds to prohibitin and ANXA2, proteins found on the surface of blood vessels that supply white adipose tissue. These proteins are abundant on the endothelial cells (blood vessel lining) in fat tissue but less common elsewhere.

**The Killing Sequence**

Once adipotide binds to its target, the second part of the molecule triggers apoptosis in those endothelial cells. The blood vessels feeding the fat tissue are destroyed. Without blood supply, the fat cells cannot receive oxygen or nutrients and they die.

**Why This Matters**

Most fat loss approaches shrink fat cells. The cells get smaller but they remain. This is why weight regain is common. When you eat more calories, those existing fat cells simply fill back up.

Adipotide actually destroys fat cells. In theory, this could produce more permanent fat loss because the cells themselves are gone. Whether this translates to better long term outcomes in humans is unknown.

**The Trade Off**

The same mechanism that makes adipotide effective also creates its primary safety concern. Destroying blood vessels anywhere in the body is risky. The targeting is not perfect. In primate studies, the most consistent side effect was mild, reversible kidney injury. This likely occurs because the kidneys are highly vascularized organs.




## Benefits

**Dramatic Fat Loss in Animal Models**

The preclinical data is impressive. Obese mice lost approximately 30% of body weight over 4 weeks. Obese rhesus monkeys lost up to 38.7% of body weight with treatment.

**Fat Cell Death Versus Shrinkage**

Adipotide kills fat cells rather than just emptying them. This is a fundamentally different approach that could theoretically lead to more lasting results.

**Metabolic Improvements**

In primate studies, adipotide improved insulin sensitivity alongside fat loss. The metabolic benefits appeared to track with the degree of weight loss.

**Different Mechanism**

For people who cannot tolerate GLP-1 drugs or have not responded to other approaches, adipotide represents a completely different pathway. It does not work through appetite suppression or metabolic rate changes.

**No Appetite Changes**

Adipotide does not affect hunger signals. You eat normally while the compound works on fat tissue directly.




## What the Science Shows

Adipotide has strong preclinical data but very limited human evidence.

**Mouse Studies (2004 onward)**

Obese mice treated with adipotide lost approximately 30% of body weight over 4 weeks. The fat loss was rapid and significant. Metabolic markers improved alongside weight loss.

# MOTS-c Mitochondrial Peptide 

## What It Is

MOTS-c is a 16 amino acid peptide that your mitochondria actually produce naturally. Unlike most peptides that are synthetic versions of hormones, MOTS-c is encoded directly in your mitochondrial DNA. Your body makes it in response to stress and exercise, and it helps regulate how your cells use energy.

The peptide was discovered in 2015 by researchers studying the mitochondrial genome. They found that when MOTS-c levels increase, it improves how your body handles glucose, enhances insulin sensitivity, and appears to slow certain aspects of aging. In animal studies, MOTS-c prevented diet induced obesity and reversed insulin resistance even in older subjects.

The interesting thing about MOTS-c is that your natural levels decline with age. This decline correlates with the metabolic dysfunction that comes with getting older: worse insulin sensitivity, more difficulty burning fat, less energy, and reduced physical capacity. Supplementing with exogenous MOTS-c may help restore some of what time takes away.

MOTS-c is available through research peptide suppliers. It has not undergone large scale human clinical trials, though a related compound (CB4211) has shown safety in early human studies.




## How It Works

MOTS-c works through a pathway called AMPK activation. AMPK is often described as your cellular energy sensor. When AMPK is activated, your cells shift toward burning fat for fuel, taking up glucose more efficiently, and producing new mitochondria.

**The AMPK Connection**

When you inject MOTS-c, it enters your cells and inhibits the folate cycle, which is connected to purine biosynthesis. This creates a kind of metabolic stress signal that activates AMPK. The result is similar to what happens when you exercise or fast: your cells become more metabolically flexible and efficient.

**Nuclear Translocation**

What makes MOTS-c unusual is that it can move from the cytoplasm into the nucleus under stress conditions. Once there, it directly regulates gene expression, particularly genes involved in the antioxidant response. This means MOTS-c is not just a metabolic signal. It is actually changing which genes get turned on.

**Skeletal Muscle Targeting**

The primary target tissue appears to be skeletal muscle. In studies, MOTS-c enhanced glucose uptake in muscle tissue, improved insulin sensitivity specifically in muscle, and helped muscle cells adapt to metabolic stress. This makes sense given that exercise naturally increases MOTS-c expression in muscle.

**Exercise Mimetic Effects**

Exercise increases your natural MOTS-c levels by about 12 fold. Researchers have described MOTS-c as an exercise mimetic because supplementing with it produces some of the same metabolic benefits as exercise: improved glucose handling, enhanced fat oxidation, and better physical performance.




## Benefits

**Improved Insulin Sensitivity**

This is the most well documented effect. In animal studies, MOTS-c treatment reversed diet induced insulin resistance and improved glucose tolerance. The effect was significant even in older animals.

**Prevention of Diet Induced Obesity**

Mice treated with MOTS-c while eating a high fat diet gained significantly less weight than untreated controls. The compound appears to enhance fat burning and prevent the metabolic dysfunction that comes with overfeeding.

**Enhanced Physical Performance**

In one study, MOTS-c treatment improved physical performance in young, middle aged, and old mice. Even when treatment started late in life (the equivalent of roughly 70 human years), mice showed improved running capacity and muscle function.

**Potential Anti-Aging Effects**

MOTS-c levels decline with age, and this decline correlates with worsening metabolic function. Restoring MOTS-c levels in older animals improved markers associated with healthy aging. Researchers have proposed it as a potential longevity intervention.

**Cardiovascular Benefits**

Animal studies show MOTS-c improves cardiac function, reduces damage from type 2 diabetes, and enhances the cardiovascular benefits of exercise.

**Reduced Inflammation**

MOTS-c downregulates inflammatory cytokines including IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α. Chronic inflammation is a driver of metabolic disease and aging, so this effect may contribute to the broader health benefits.

**Bone Health**

Recent research shows MOTS-c promotes osteoblast proliferation and inhibits osteoclast production, suggesting potential benefits for bone metabolism.




## What the Science Shows

MOTS-c has substantial preclinical data but limited human evidence.

**Initial Discovery Study (2015)**

Published in Cell Metabolism by Lee et al. This landmark paper identified MOTS-c and demonstrated that it regulates insulin sensitivity and metabolic homeostasis in mice. Treatment prevented both age dependent and high fat diet induced insulin resistance, as well as diet induced obesity.

Unknown. Large scale human safety data does not exist. The fact that MOTS-c is naturally produced by your body is encouraging, but exogenous supplementation may have different effects than endogenous production.

# 5-Amino-1MQ (NNMT Inhibitor) 




## What It Is

5-Amino-1MQ is a small molecule compound that blocks an enzyme called NNMT (nicotinamide N-methyltransferase). This enzyme plays a central role in how your body stores and burns fat. When NNMT activity is high, your metabolism shifts toward fat storage. When you inhibit NNMT, the opposite happens: your body becomes better at burning fat for fuel.

Unlike most compounds in this series, 5-Amino-1MQ is not a peptide. It is a small molecule, which means it has different properties. Most notably, it survives your digestive system intact and can be taken orally with approximately 38% bioavailability. Subcutaneous injection bypasses the digestive system entirely, so more reaches your bloodstream at the same dose.

The compound was developed by researchers at the University of Texas Medical Branch who were looking for new ways to treat obesity and metabolic dysfunction. In animal studies, mice treated with 5-Amino-1MQ lost significant body fat without any reduction in food intake. The fat loss came entirely from increased energy expenditure at the cellular level. This is a fundamentally different mechanism than GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide, which work primarily by suppressing appetite.

5-Amino-1MQ is a research compound that has not been approved by the FDA. Human clinical trial data is limited, though the preclinical evidence is promising.




## How It Works

Your mitochondria are the power plants inside your cells. They take the food you eat and convert it into usable energy called ATP. NAD+ is essential for that process. Think of NAD+ as a shuttle that picks up electrons from the food you eat and carries them into the electron transport chain. This cycle repeats thousands of times per day in every cell. If you don't have enough NAD+ available, your cells can't efficiently shuttle electrons, which means less ATP and less energy.

Your body makes NAD+ primarily through the salvage pathway, which is responsible for about 80% of your NAD+ production. But your body also has an enzyme called NNMT that takes nicotinamide (a form of vitamin B3) and methylates it into a waste product that gets excreted. This is your body's way of clearing out excess B3.

Here's where the problem starts. You now have two pathways competing for the same resource. One turns nicotinamide into NAD+ (energy), and the other driven by NNMT turns nicotinamide into waste.

Under normal conditions, this isn't an issue. But in your adipose tissue (fat cells), NAD+ synthesis primarily relies on the salvage pathway, and NNMT is the only catabolic enzyme for nicotinamide in fat. When NNMT gets overexpressed in adipose tissue, which happens with obesity, it creates a drain on the one pathway fat cells have for making NAD+.

This creates a vicious cycle: obesity increases NNMT expression in fat tissue, which depletes NAD+ in fat cells, which impairs fat burning and promotes fat storage, which leads to more fat, which means more NNMT.

5-Amino-1MQ plugs that leak. It blocks NNMT, and by blocking that enzyme in adipose tissue, it stops the waste and shifts the balance back toward NAD+ production.

When you inhibit NNMT with 5-Amino-1MQ:

- NAD+ levels increase because less nicotinamide gets wasted
- SAM levels increase because less gets consumed by NNMT
- SIRT1 (a longevity protein) gets activated due to higher NAD+
- Fat cells shrink and lipogenesis (new fat creation) decreases
- Energy expenditure increases without changes to appetite or food intake

The key point is that 5-Amino-1MQ does not make you eat less. It makes your cells burn more energy.




## Who This Is Actually For

This is important: the research shows that NNMT overexpression correlates more with body fat than with aging. The impressive results from the research were in diet-induced obese mice. Those mice had elevated NNMT to begin with. Blocking it corrected a dysfunction that was already present.

In lean individuals, adipose NNMT is at baseline levels. There's no pathological overexpression creating a drain on NAD+. If there's no dysfunction, there's nothing to correct.

The target population based on the mechanism is people with metabolic dysfunction tied to excess body fat, not lean people looking to optimize.

If you're lean and metabolically healthy, your NNMT probably isn't overexpressed and there's not much dysfunction to correct. Consider whether this compound makes sense for your situation.

## Dosing Protocol

**Why Most Dosing Protocols Online Don't Make Sense**

The mouse study used doses that translate to roughly 400 mg per day for humans using allometric scaling. For a drug to actually do its job, you need enough of it in your bloodstream to block at least 50% of the target enzyme. This is called IC50 coverage. If you're below that threshold, the drug isn't doing much.

Microdoses of 150 to 600 micrograms per day get you roughly 400 times below the threshold needed for 50% inhibition. You can't outsmart pharmacology. Either you're achieving effective concentration or you're not.
